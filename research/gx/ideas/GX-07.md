# GX-07: Test a map portfolio on a held-out side

The x3 counter experiment shows the upside of selecting a strategy for a deterministic map and side. Its same-opponent result cannot show whether the strategy transfers. The strong x3mf default remains the comparison.

Test uniform arms 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 17, 19, 25 and 29 on the full pool and all three opponents. These cover distinct early territory, fight, banking, and late posture choices from the existing x3 palette. Use the same executable sections for every arm; the selector reads volatile data. Arm 0 retains the x3mf settings with the selector running and measures the overhead of compiling it in.

Fit only on side A across all three opponents. A map can switch when an arm improves at least two wins, adds at most one fuel-out, and has zero faults on its three training games. Resolve ties by banked margin, then lower fuel and arm number. Apply the same arm on both sides. Side B outcomes are out of sample. If the side B gains do not carry over, keep the selector out even when the combined score looks good. Also compare the selected release to x3mf and run both generated sets against v92 and x1bru.
