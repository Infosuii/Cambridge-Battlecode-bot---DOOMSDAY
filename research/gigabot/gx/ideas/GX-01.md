# GX-01: Price the return leg in camp selection

The ladder audit estimates that x3's predecessors converted 69.6% of collected gems into banked score in losses, versus 91.9% for their opponents. The existing route scorer pays for the walk to each camp, respawn wait, combat and capture. The altar walk is usually paid later, outside that ranking. For example, the team collected 1,107 gems and banked 210 in an audited loss on adgato_gemcombat4.

First test: charge 30% of the endpoint's existing terrain distance to a friendly altar, capped at 32 steps before scaling. Pay once at the end of a multi-camp route. This approximates an amortized return across several captures, without adding a field build or changing when tribute starts. No charge when the altar field is unavailable, the map cannot bank, or the route yields no gems. Gold-only jobs retain their old score when they carry no new gems.

Fuel: one bounded lookup per eligible route endpoint. Use the existing scorer and route-search budget checks. If added work harms dense maps, replace per-candidate lookups with a cached distance at the camp representative or restrict the calculation to a shortlist.

Compare all 516 pool games to x3mf; check banked/collected rates, fuel and deaths alongside paired wins. On a weak first result, try a smaller return charge or a risk-dependent marginal return term before deciding whether to retain the mechanism.

Fixed charge result: 271/516 versus 341/516 (78 gains, 148 losses, z = −4.66). Fuel-outs were unchanged at 548; faults were zero. Collected gems fell 6.2%, banked gems fell 7.5%, and deaths rose from 10.61 to 11.61 per game. The rate discount removed too many productive contested camps. The next variant only charges an increase in altar distance when the champion already holds 24 or more gems. It charges no return leg to an empty farmer, so it preserves opening camp valuation.
